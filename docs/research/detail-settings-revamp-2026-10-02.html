NuvioTV Detail & Settings Revamp

I looked at how the official Nuvio apps, the other Apple TV ports of Nuvio and the big tvOS media apps lay out their title pages and settings. This board compares them with NuvioTV today and sets out four directions for each screen, plus the smaller pieces you can mix in. Each direction comes with a rough mockup, what it fixes, and what it costs.

Starting point

NuvioTV today

Detail page

  • Full-bleed backdrop with an optional poster on the right 40%, a muted hero trailer and a fixed scrim.
  • Top block: logo, meta chips, the action row (Play + 4 glass buttons), genres, and a long label/value "Details" table (Director … Awards, Ratings) in a 1200 pt left column. A 560 pt glass synopsis panel sits on the right, capped at 18 lines.
  • Then nine stacked rows: studio logos, parental guide, episodes, cast, saga, trailers, More Like This, comments.
  • Ratings appear only as one IMDb chip and a "Ratings" line in the Details table. There is no rating control, no tabs and no way to hide sections.
  • DetailView.swift is 2,570 lines.
FEAT-35 "looks a bit dated"BUG-127 panel is interimBUG-96/99 scroll anchoringBUG-41 glass jank

Settings

  • Sidebar of 7 categories plus a native List on the right. Moving focus over a category swaps the pane without a click. That part works well.
  • The panes are lopsided: Playback ≈29 rows, Appearance ≈33, About ≈28, Advanced 2.
  • The Detail page options are buried in Appearance › Poster Style.
  • About shows about 20 diagnostics and A/B switches to every user.
  • There is no search, no setting descriptions and no live previews (except the colour swatches). There is no Clear cache, Licenses or Privacy row.
  • Settings Style has Default and Minimal. The Top Bar style Steven asked for in FEAT-7 never shipped.

Patterns that keep coming up on Detail

  • The landing view is a quiet hero: logo bottom-left, one meta line, a short synopsis, Play. Everything else is below the fold.
  • Focus lands on Play/Resume, and the logo has a fixed slot so nothing reflows when data arrives.
  • Technical badges (4K, HDR, DV, Atmos) sit on one line in a secondary tone.
  • Episodes first for series, then cast, extras, similar, and the About/Details block last.
  • The page can be configured: a layout choice (Infuse, Swiftfin), per-row toggles (AF2, Orivio, Nuvio mobile).
  • The backdrop dims or blurs as you scroll down.

Patterns that keep coming up in Settings

  • One title above a split view: categories on the left, a grouped list on the right.
  • The setting under focus is explained in place (Swiftfin's left pane, Orivio's subtitles).
  • Three to six groups at the top, diagnostics last and usually hidden.
  • Live previews for anything visual (bobsupra, official NuvioTV, AF2).
  • Native list rows. Glass only on controls, never on the list background (HIG; Orivio's focus-trap lesson).

A · first screen. Rows peek at the bottom; Down brings them up while the backdrop dims.

A · Cinematic Clean Recommended base

The Apple TV app layout from Steven's photo. The first screen is just the hero. The left block becomes logo, genre line, a 3–4 line synopsis, a badge line, then the buttons. Cast and director move to a small right-aligned credits block. The 18-line glass panel and the long Details table go away; the table moves to an About section at the bottom of the page. The rows below stay as they are, but the first one only peeks until you press Down.

For

  • It answers FEAT-35 and BUG-127 directly, matching his own reference photo.
  • Removes the biggest glass surface, which was the main BUG-41 cost.
  • The rows and the scroll anchoring below the fold barely change.

Against

  • A long synopsis gets cut to 4 lines. It needs a "More" press or a focusable synopsis, as in official NuvioTV and Orivio.
  • Add-on titles without a backdrop look flat. Fall back to option D's ambient look.

B · the whole page fits on one screen. The tab strip swaps the row underneath it.

B · Tabbed Shelf

The Netflix layout from Steven's second photo, and what official NuvioTV does for Cast, Ratings, More Like This, Trailer and Collection. The hero stays at the top, and under it a single tab strip (Episodes, Cast & Crew, More Like This, Trailers & Extras, Details, Comments) swaps one shelf underneath. The page hardly scrolls.

For

  • Steven asked for tabs by name in FEAT-35.
  • With little or no vertical scroll, the BUG-96/99 anchoring problems mostly go away.
  • Every section is one press from the hero.

Against

  • Content behind a tab is easy to miss. Saga and comments get used less.
  • Focus between the tab strip and the shelf is a known tvOS trouble spot: it needs a focus guide and must remember the last tab.
  • The biggest rewrite of the four.

D · built from the poster. Works for add-on titles that have no backdrop.

D · Poster & Ambient Colour

Steven's "large poster with a shadow" request, combined with NuvioMobile's Dominant Color background mode. The page is tinted with a blurred wash of the poster's own colours, and a large poster card sits beside the information. This is the same colour extraction as T3 "Depth Takes Poster Color".

For

  • Looks good for anime, add-on and older titles whose backdrops are missing or poor.
  • Every title gets its own colour, which suits the poster-colour features already built.

Against

  • Less cinematic than A for big releases with good key art.
  • Best as a background mode (Cinematic / Ambient / Auto) on top of A, not as the only layout.

1 · the left side explains whatever row has focus.

1 · Native Split + Explainer Recommended base

The tvOS Settings app and Swiftfin's layout. One "Settings" title sits over a split. The right side keeps today's native list. The left side changes with focus: on the category list it shows a large icon and a short summary of the category; inside a pane it shows the focused row's icon and a plain description of what that setting does. To keep the category rail, use a third, narrow icon column (Steven's FEAT-45).

For

  • Fixes the real problem with ~120 settings: nobody knows what "Network Readahead" or "Keep Profile 7 FEL" does.
  • Closest to Apple's own design. Fits the HIG contract.
  • Every row needs a description written once, and that text also becomes the docs.

Against

  • Writing ~120 descriptions is the real cost.
  • The list gets narrower, so long values need short labels.

2 · Grouped Rail + Live Preview

Official NuvioTV's grouped rail (YOU, LOOK, WATCH, SERVICES, SYSTEM), each category with a one-line subtitle as in Orivio. Panes that change how things look get a third column with a live preview card that updates as you scroll through values. This is what Steven asked for in FEAT-41.

3 · categories across the top. The pane uses the full width in two columns.

3 · Horizon Top Tabs

Official NuvioTV's "Horizon" style and the Top Bar choice Steven asked for in FEAT-7. Categories become a capsule bar at the top with a pill that slides between them. Below it, the pane splits into two columns of section groups, so a 29-row pane fits on about one screen.

For

  • Matches the app's Top Tabs navigation option.
  • Uses the screen width. Short panes look less empty.
  • Easy to offer as a third Settings Style next to Default and Minimal.

Against

  • Seven or more tabs in a capsule is crowded. Labels must stay short.
  • Two list columns break the native single-list focus model. Left/Right between them needs care.

4 · a status board. Each tile opens its pane.

4 · Status Hub

The opening screen is a grid of large tiles. Each one shows the current state in a few words (Trakt connected, mpv, Hero on), like bobsupra's account stats strips. Opening a tile pushes a full-screen list. It works like a dashboard of the setup.

For

  • You see how the app is set up at a glance. Useful when helping a tester ("send a photo of Settings").
  • Very approachable for new users.

Against

  • One extra press to every setting, and the instant pane preview is lost.
  • The status lines are a second source of truth that has to be kept current.

Under any Settings option

Re-sort the categories first

This is the cheapest win and none of the directions works without it. Today two panes carry most of the rows and the Detail page settings are hard to find.

Today (7)

  1. Account & Services ≈27 rows
  2. Playback ≈29 rows: player, skipping, sources, subtitles, languages
  3. Appearance ≈33 rows: theme, posters, Detail page, depth, badges
  4. Home Screen ≈13 rows + catalogs
  5. Content Sources ≈14 rows + plugins
  6. Advanced 2 rows
  7. About ≈28 rows, about 20 of them diagnostics

Proposed (9, in 4 groups)

  1. You · Account & Profiles sign in, server, Remote Setup
  2. You · Services Trakt, Simkl, MDBList, Debrid
  3. Look · Appearance theme, posters, focus, depth, badges
  4. Look · Home Screen hero, rows, catalogs
  5. Look · Detail Page new: layout, background, trailer, sections
  6. Watch · Player engine, frame rate, buffer, skipping
  7. Watch · Sources auto-play, filters, sort, plugins, metadata
  8. Watch · Subtitles & Audio style, languages, SDH
  9. System · About version, Clear cache, Licenses. Diagnostics hidden behind a "Developer" switch, unlocked by pressing Version several times

Also worth considering

  • Essential / Advanced mode (official NuvioTV, Orivio): hide the power-user rows (Profile 7 FEL, readahead, the A/B switches) unless Advanced is on.
  • Settings search (NuvioMobile indexes every row). Typing with the Siri Remote is slow and the HIG contract rules out .searchable, so this is low priority on TV.
  • A settings reference doc like Orivio's SETTINGS_REFERENCE.md, which would double as the source of the option 1 descriptions.
If I had to pick

Recommendation

Detail: build A · Cinematic Clean as the new default. Add D's ambient colour as a Background setting (Cinematic / Ambient / Auto, where Auto picks Ambient when there is no backdrop). Take the cheap add-ons first: fixed logo slot, ratings strip, About block last, section toggles. Keep B's tabs in reserve. If Steven still finds the page too long once A ships, the tabs can replace only the rows below the fold, without touching the hero.

Settings: re-sort the categories into the 9-pane layout first, then move to 1 · Native Split + Explainer, with option 2's live preview only on Appearance (FEAT-41) and Home. Offer Horizon (option 3) later as the Top Bar Settings Style if testers still want it.

Ship it like Infuse did: keep the current Detail layout as a "Classic" choice for one or two betas, so testers can compare the two on their own TVs.

Ground rules from the research, whichever option you pick: glass only behind controls and never wrapping focusable content (Orivio's focus trap, HIG). Focus always lands on Play. Nothing moves once data loads. Stay inside the 60/80 pt safe area so focused cards do not overlap text.

Questions for you

How far should the Detail page go? A refines the current structure. B (tabs) is a different page. Which kind of change are you after?
Should the old Detail layout survive? Infuse-style opt-in, or a clean switch?
Should Settings get more categories? Going from 7 to 9 makes each pane smaller but makes the list longer.
Who writes the setting descriptions? Option 1 needs about 120 short descriptions in plain words. I can draft them from the code.
Should the diagnostics stay visible to testers? Steven's photo loop depends on them. A Developer switch keeps them one step away instead of removing them.
